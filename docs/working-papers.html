Monetary policy, banking and macro-finance

  • The Heterogeneous Bank Lending Channel of Monetary Policy
    Galo Nuño Barrau, Jorge Abad, Saki Bigio, Salomon Garcia-Villegas, Joël Marbet
    NBER WP 35239; CEPR DP 21519; Banco de España WP 2620

    Quantitative model of heterogeneous banks: differences in interest-rate risk exposure (fixed- vs variable-rate lending) matter for monetary transmission only when bank solvency concerns bind.

  • Monetary Policy with Supply Regimes
    Galo Nuño Barrau, Philipp Renner, Simon Scheidegger
    CEPR DP 19678; SSRN · R&R, Journal of Monetary Economics

    Globally optimal monetary policy in a New Keynesian model with regimes of persistently higher costs (tariffs, wars, fragmentation): commitment tolerates temporary inflation, discretion creates an inflationary bias.

  • Firm Heterogeneity, Capital Misallocation and Optimal Monetary Policy
    Galo Nuño Barrau, Beatriz González, Dominik Thaler
    CEPR DP 18695; ECB WP 2890; BIS WP 1148 · R&R, Journal of Political Economy

    Capital misallocation across heterogeneous firms with financial frictions creates a new expansion motive for monetary policy, but approximate price stability remains optimal except after cost-push shocks.

  • Decomposing Optimal Debt Maturity
    Galo Nuño Barrau, Saki Bigio
    R&R, AEJ: Macroeconomics

    A value-gap rule decomposes optimal sovereign debt maturity into hedging, self-insurance, credit-risk and dilution forces; hedging dominates and the model recommends longer maturities.

  • Asset Pricing with Heterogeneous Agents under Limited Information
    Galo Nuño Barrau, Riccardo Cioffi, Samuel Hurtado

    Two-way interaction between wealth inequality and asset prices in a heterogeneous-agent model with aggregate risk, solved with neural networks, generating endogenous time-varying risk premia.

  • Planning against Disasters in Dynamic Production Networks
    Galo Nuño Barrau, Vasco M. Carvalho, Matias Covarrubias
    CESifo WP 12796

    In nonlinear multisector production networks the planner over-invests in upstream sectors to mitigate cascading disasters, implying a much larger welfare cost of business cycles (solved with deep learning).

  • The Redistributive Effects of Sectoral Shocks
    Ghassane Benmir, Côme Poirier

    Heterogeneous-agent input-output model showing how sectoral shocks propagate through production networks and redistribute income and wealth.

Climate economics, carbon pricing and the green transition

  • Green Asset Pricing
    Ghassane Benmir, Ivan Jaccard, Gauthier Vermandel
    ECB WP 2477 · R&R, Journal of Finance (per CV)

    Empirically grounding the stochastic discount factor raises the optimal carbon price by 32% and makes it far more procyclical, reducing risk premia and precautionary saving.

  • Weitzman Meets Taylor: EU Allowance Price Drivers and Carbon Cap Rules
    Ghassane Benmir, Josselin Roman, Luca Taschini
    Grantham Research Institute WP 421 · Submitted

    Estimated cap-and-trade DSGE on EU ETS data: abatement-cost shocks drive permit-price volatility; a Taylor-rule-like carbon cap rule cuts volatility from over 40% to under 4%.

  • Beneath the Trees: The Influence of Natural Capital on Shadow Price Dynamics in a Macroeconomic Model with Uncertainty
    Ghassane Benmir, Aditya Mori, Josselin Roman, Romano Tarsia
    Grantham Research Institute WP 420 · Submitted

    DSGE with renewable and non-renewable natural capital degraded by temperature: natural capital raises the social cost of carbon by about 10% relative to DICE, much more under shocks.

  • The Distributional Costs of Net-Zero: A Heterogeneous Agent Perspective
    Ghassane Benmir, Josselin Roman

    Heterogeneous-agent model of the US 2050 net-zero target: long-run welfare gain but a 6-10% rise in financially constrained households; carbon-revenue redistribution smooths the transition.

  • Unveiling the Green Equity Premium: A Macro-Financial Outlook
    Ghassane Benmir, Rhys Bidder, Simone Maso, Aditya Mori, Josselin Roman

    New greenness classification based on EU ETS exposure: greener Euro-area assets earn a positive premium when carbon prices are low that shrinks as policy commitment rises; climate-sentiment shocks drive returns.

  • Policy Interactions and the Transition to Clean Technology
    Ghassane Benmir, Akash Raja, Josselin Roman

    Two-sector green/brown DSGE with financial frictions: macroprudential climate risk-weights and green-tilted QE can close the welfare wedge and risk-premium distortion created by the EU ETS.

  • Carbon Pricing with Regressive Co-benefits: Evidence from British Columbia's Carbon Tax
    Lorenzo Sileci
    Grantham Research Institute WP 405 · Under review

    BC's 2008 carbon tax cut PM2.5 emissions by 5-11% with large monetised co-benefits, but widened pollution-exposure disparities across income and racial groups.

  • More than just carbon: the socioeconomic co-benefits of large-scale tree planting
    Lorenzo Sileci, Jeffrey Pagel, Charles Palmer
    Grantham Research Institute WP 410 · Under review

    Philippines' National Greening Program (2011-2018): large-scale tree planting reduced local poverty by about 6 pp, shifted labour out of agriculture and sequestered 71-303 MtCO2.

  • Board Gender Diversity and Environmental Performance: Association or Pre-existing Firm Dynamics?
    Ainara González de San Román, Patricia Gabaldón, Liliana Gelabert
    SSRN (earlier title: Unveiling the Myth: A Causal Reassessment of Gender Diversity's Impact on Corporate Environmental Performance) · Under review, Journal of Corporate Finance

    S&P 500 panel 2010-2020: the positive association between women on boards and environmental performance disappears under staggered DiD because firms go green before appointing women directors.

Fertility, Ageing, Demographic Transition and Health

  • Freed from the Boys: How Single-Sex Schooling Shapes Girls' Effort and Performance in High-Stakes Exams
    Daniel Fernández Kranz, Caterina Calsamiglia, Yarine Fawaz, Junhee Lee
    IZA DP 18208; CEPR DP 21477 · Submitted

    Lottery-based school assignment: girls placed in single-sex schools exert more effort and outperform boys in high-stakes math exams, at the cost of higher stress and worse mental health.

  • Maternity Benefits, Consumption and Labor Supply: Estimating Causal Effects with Bank Transaction Data
    Daniel Fernández Kranz, Libertad González, Alberto Graziano, Josep Mestres
    AEA 2026 conference paper

    Regression discontinuity with bank transaction data: a large cash transfer to new mothers in Madrid raised consumption of liquidity-constrained women without reducing their labor earnings.

  • Private Schooling and Fertility Decline in India
    Daniel Fernández Kranz, Trong-Anh Trinh
    IZA DP 18771 · Reject and resubmit, Journal of Population Economics

    The expansion of fee-based private schooling raised the cost of child quality and, interacting with son preference, generated gender-differentiated fertility stopping that contributed to India's fertility decline.

  • Childhood Exposure to Joint Custody Reforms and Adult Family Formation
    Daniel Fernández Kranz, Sébastien Fontenay
    IZA DP 18831

    Staggered US joint-custody reforms and 13 million ACS observations: childhood exposure reduces adult fertility by 7 percent for both women and men, mainly via lower parenthood and couple formation.

  • The 'Superwoman Effect' of Female Representation in Animated Series on Gender Roles and Educational Achievements
    Sasha Cheipesh, Warn N. Lekfuangfu

    TV-signal coverage in early-2000s Ukraine: girls exposed to female-led superhero cartoons attain more education, choose STEM more often and hold less traditional gender stereotypes.

  • Just the Flu? The Labor Market Spillovers of Influenza Vaccination
    Sasha Cheipesh
    Working paper (job market paper)

    Difference-in-differences using variation in US flu-vaccine quality and local vaccination rates: influenza vaccination raises employment and wages, with productivity, demand and sectoral spillover channels.

  • Thinking, Fast and Slow: How Response Times Can Predict Cognitive Decline and (Bad) Financial Decision-Making at Older Ages
    Sasha Cheipesh, Yarine Fawaz, Pedro Mira
    Working paper (presented at NBER Summer Institute 2025)

    Interview response times in SHARE predict subsequent cognitive decline, health deterioration and wealth losses among older Europeans, especially for those unaware of their own decline.

  • Income, Employment and Health Risks of Older Workers
    Siqi Wei
    CEMFI WP 2205

    Using the US HRS, models older workers' transitions to bridge jobs and retirement, separating health, productivity, mismatch and job-destruction risks, and quantifies the welfare cost of mismatch risk.

  • Estimating Choice Models with Piecewise Smooth Objective Functions: Application to Joint Retirement
    Siqi Wei, Tatiana Rosá
    SSRN

    Method for estimating choice models with piecewise smooth objective functions (stochastic EM), applied to joint retirement of European couples; finds complementarity between spouses in exiting the labor force.

Education, technology and public policy

  • From Pilot to Policy: Experimental Evidence from Scaling Online Tutoring
    Toni Roldán, María Calle, Lucas Gortazar, María Hernández-de-Benito, Claudia Hupkau, Teresa Molina-Millán
    CEP Discussion Paper 2176 (LSE); EdWorkingPaper 26-1496 · Submitted

    RCT of an online math tutoring programme scaled up by regional education authorities in Spain: effects are about one-third of the pilot's, quantifying the voltage drop when scaling evidence-based interventions.

  • Digital Infrastructure and Work Organization: Evidence from Spain's FTTH Rollout
    Daniel Fernández Kranz, Liliana Gelabert
    SSRN 6644231

    Causal effects of fiber-to-the-home broadband expansion in Spain, exploiting its staggered provincial rollout 2012-2023, on how work is organized and how households allocate time.

  • A Collective Review on Some Potential Negative Impacts of Smartphone and Social Media Use on Adolescent Mental Health: Results from a Delphi Process
    Daniel Fernández Kranz, Valerio Capraro, Laura Globig, Zachary Rausch, Steve Rathje and 120+ others
    SSRN 5256747 · Submitted

    Delphi consensus among 120+ researchers from 11 disciplines on adolescent mental-health trends, causal links to smartphone and social media use, and policy recommendations.

Development economics

  • Does Performance Evidence Motivate? A Field Experiment in Guinea-Bissau's Health Sector
    Mattia Fracchia
    Working paper (Weiss Family Distinguished Paper, NEUDC 2023)

    RCT with Guinea-Bissau's Ministry of Health: giving frontline health workers credible evidence of their programme's impact raised time in direct patient care by 39%.

  • For Honor or for Profit? An Experiment on Recruiting Traditional Health Practitioners for Formal Healthcare in Guinea-Bissau
    Mattia Fracchia, Brais Álvarez-Pereira, Teresa Molina-Millán, Pedro C. Vicente
    NOVAFRICA WP 2502 · R&R, World Bank Economic Review

    Randomised honor vs. profit recruitment messages for training traditional health practitioners as formal health agents; the profit message raises interest, especially among less religious practitioners.
